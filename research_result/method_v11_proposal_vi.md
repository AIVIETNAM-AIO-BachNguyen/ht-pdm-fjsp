# V11 proposal: reference-continuation long-horizon action credit for RL dispatch

Written before V11 implementation or outcomes. V10's full selected-policy DEV cost was 878.22 versus bounded rollout 849.34 and risk 894.61; its within-machine-set assignment-credit ablation was only −4.03, CI95 [−11.64,+0.46]. The independently audited first-divergence probe on already-opened V10 DEV tapes found a more specific gap: in reciprocal scale2, V10 served where rollout WAITed at 58/80 first divergences; in interchangeable scale1, first differences split between machine set (29/80) and service versus WAIT (26/80). Six-tick first-root costs were nearly tied (+0.10/+2.50 V10 minus rollout), but full-H64 costs under **the same risk continuation** were +40.41/+57.36, positive on average in all five training seeds. This supports delayed competition/failure credit across WAIT, machine priority and assignment, not a hard service rule.

## Formulation and mechanism

The same centralized V7 shared-edge actor observes the current 56-D, current-information threshold-admitted system state and selects one full legal machine–technician matching, including WAIT, with no risk policy, forecast or future tape at inference. Requests, feasibility, four N4/K2 cases, H48+tail16 simulator and objective stay frozen. At scratch-RL training states collected from the current stochastic actor, enumerate all legal roots and draw a paired common physical-scenario bank. For each root estimate two undiscounted H+tail costs on the *same* bank:

`Q_self(s,a)`: force the root, then continue the current actor stochastically until H48 and the fixed risk tail to H64 (the V9/V10 estimator).

`Q_ref(s,a)`: force the root, then continue the **fixed risk-matching policy** until H64. This is a training-only reference continuation, never a teacher action label or runtime dispatcher.

The V11 actor receives an all-action expected policy-gradient update using centered `Q_ref` return advantages. The matched ablation receives the **same complete Q_self and Q_ref banks and simulation compute** but updates from centered `Q_self` alone. Both use the exact same actor, optimizer/KL control, source-state sampling, initial weights, training/evaluation budgets and current feasible actions; only the return-continuation estimator differs. `Q_ref` evaluates the delayed cost of a current technician commitment or deferral under a stable current-information policy. It may expose WAIT and priority opportunity values that a weak current actor continuation obscures. This is model-based scratch RL: physical return supplies action credit and the actor learns the full dispatch; no imitation, pretraining, hard mask, planner decision label, cost shaping or physics change is used.

Known policy improvement with a baseline rollout, action-integrated expected gradients, and maintenance graph RL are established prior ideas (see primary literature links and distinctions in `method_v9_proposal_vi.md`). V11 does **not** claim to invent model-based rollouts or reference-policy evaluation. The tested design distinction is a common-bank **full-H+tail reference-continuation critic for every legal joint maintenance matching** in a persistent shared-technician system, isolated against an own-policy-continuation critic at equal compute. Any contribution claim requires a broad seedwise ablation gain and strong baseline result; an intuitive mechanism alone is insufficient.

## Falsifiable hypotheses and risks

H1: reference-continuation credit improves four-case economic cost by ≥10 versus equal-compute own-continuation credit, across ≥4/5 fresh training seeds and not only weak replicas. H2: target-case H failures and full-episode cost fall, with no hidden case collapse, and learned root action changes are timing/machine-priority choices supported by long-horizon values. H3: runtime remains ≥10× faster than bounded rollout and the result approaches planner cost without a simple heuristic dominating both cost and speed. Falsification includes no robust ablation gain, merely imitating risk (which already loses to rollout), or overoptimistic/biased Q_ref leading to poor closed-loop states. Finite scenario variance and actor representation remain limitations. No generalization or novelty claim is made before tests.
