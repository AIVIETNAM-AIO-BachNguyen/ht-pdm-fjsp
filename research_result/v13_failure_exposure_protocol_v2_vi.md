# V13 at-risk preventive exposure probe, revised after structural smoke finding

This prospectively locks a revised diagnostic **before full DEV event replay**. The prior `v13_failure_event_protocol_vi.md` and its 16-episode smoke artifact are preserved. That smoke replay and source inspection found a structural identity: with the fixed threshold requester, a machine reaches failure age only after a preventive request has been admitted, and physical service prevents failure during the interval. Thus every observed H48 failure in that smoke was a preventive-request `upgraded` event; newly created corrective events and residual failures were zero. The original upgrade-versus-new-corrective gate is therefore non-discriminating and will not be used for full DEV interpretation. This revision follows a known structural fact and precedes any full DEV event replay. No V14 partial score, fresh TEST, new physical seed or new controller is involved.

## Distinguishing question and frozen panel

The audited V13 competition policy has almost the same total H48 starts as bounded rollout but about 2.2-2.4 more H48 failures per episode in interchangeable scale1 and reciprocal scale2. Does it leave preventive requests exposed to possible failure for more machine-ticks, and how much of that exposure occurs when an immediate service start is individually legal? Replay the same selected V13 competition checkpoints for five training seeds 72500000–04 on the same 16 already opened DEV physical seeds 72510000–15 in all four cases. Replay bounded rollout once per case/physical seed and duplicate it only in paired statistics. H48 actor and planner decisions, threshold request admission, physical tape and H16 risk tail remain unchanged. Reconcile every episode with immutable V13 `episodes.csv` and source/checkpoint hashes.

At H48 tick `t`, after deterministic request admission and before matching: a machine is *at risk* if it has an unstarted preventive request, is not failed, and `age + 1 >= failure_age`. An at-risk machine contributes one **exposed machine-tick** if the chosen matching does not start its service at `t`. An exposed tick is **individually feasible** if at least one legal matching available at `t` starts that machine; otherwise it is **capacity constrained**. Individual feasibility does not imply all simultaneous at-risk machines could be served, nor that serving one would avert a later failure. Record a served-at-risk count too. Independently count lifecycle starts/upgrades, age/wait at preventive start/upgrade, failure events, H48 operating metrics and H64 cost.

## Locked metrics and gate

Primary: candidate-minus-rollout mean exposed machine-ticks per episode for interchangeable scale1 and reciprocal scale2, separately. A descriptive excess-exposure signal requires mean difference at least +3 in **each** target case, positive difference in at least 4/5 training seeds in each case, and lower 95% paired hierarchical CI >0 in each case. Bootstrap 20,000 draws with seed 72540001, resampling five training seeds, then shared 16 physical indices within each seed. Report per-seed and per-case differences, not only the joint gate. This is opened DEV after checkpoint selection and is not confirmatory evidence.

Secondary: exposed ticks individually feasible versus capacity constrained, their proportions; at-risk served starts; PM/CM H48 service starts; preventive start and upgrade age/wait distributions; H48 failures and availability; H64 objective/failure cost; first failure tick and paired machine/tick overlap. Retain old upgrade/new-corrective/residual counts explicitly to show the structural identity. Interpret feasible exposure as a decision opportunity, constrained exposure as resource occupancy, and both only as observational trajectory accounting. The signal does not establish a causal action improvement or justify a new architecture by itself. All four cases, including reciprocal3, are reported without selecting favorable cases after seeing results.
