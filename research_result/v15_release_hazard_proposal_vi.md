# V15 proposal — learned release-horizon PM exposure in matching scores

Status: **proposal and falsifiable diagnostic plan only**. V14 identity-specific control failed independently audited DEV; its unopened replication/TEST blocks are retired. No V15 training, new seed registry or TEST has begun. Before implementation, first run a prelocked representability/value probe on already opened V14 training Q states, then freeze a separate fresh-seed scratch-RL protocol if that probe supports the mechanism. The original goal's A/B, five-seed, replication and single fresh confirmation requirements remain unchanged.

## 1. Specific failure and evidence

V14's equal-capacity identity term changes cost by only −1.72 versus count-calibrated demand (CI95[−7.08,+2.80]) and changes just 39/7,680 old-policy training-state choices when toggled at identical weights; H1 fails. Nevertheless the joint actor's four-case DEV cost is 0.53% above bounded rollout at 29.2× lower selection time. Its case deficits are +47.06 in interchangeable and +48.84 in reciprocal2, where it has about two more H48 failures and far more queued PM ticks than rollout, while reciprocal3 improves. V13's audited first-at-risk capacity exposure was real, but deleting one earlier service edge did not improve target costs; the actor reissued every deleted service, usually by the originally constrained tick. These facts motivate a repeated **action-conditioned forecast of compatible technician release before PM failure**, not another unserved-machine identity penalty, a hard hold rule, or a one-time oracle correction.

## 2. Idea, formulation and training mechanism

At every H48 decision, score every currently legal partial matching, including WAIT. For candidate `a`, let `R_j(a)` be the known number of ticks until technician `j` is free: its current residual service ticks if busy; the service time of `(m,j)` if newly assigned in `a`; otherwise zero. For each unfailed, not-currently-serviced machine `n`, define the earliest compatible release `D_n(a)=min_{j: service_time[n,j]>0} R_j(a)`. Let `g_n=max(0,failure_age-age_n-1)`. If `n` is not started by `a`, its unavoidable-at-this-root exposed interval before any compatible technician is free is `h_n(a)=max(0,D_n(a)-g_n)` and its **model-implied** probability of at least one failure shock in that interval is `Z_n(a)=1-(1-p)^{h_n(a)}`. If `n` starts in `a`, set `Z_n(a)=0`. The calculation uses only the known current state, compatibility, durations and configured Bernoulli hazard, never the realized future tape. It is a local lower-bound exposure proxy, because later actor choices can still delay service after first release.

Use the V14 count-calibrated actor as a shared base score. Its existing learned nonnegative machine-technician demand is aggregated into a positive learned machine weight `w_n(s)` over compatible technicians. Add a trainable positive global scale shared by both arms. The proposed root logit is `L_base(a) - alpha * Σ_n w_n(s)[Z_n(a)-Z_n(WAIT)]`; WAIT's incremental term is zero. Negative increments for starting an exposed PM machine can reward prevention; positive increments price technician occupation that temporarily strands another machine. Recompute the feature at every current tick; the policy has no persistent reservation state and imposes no new feasibility mask. Train all weights from scratch with the same V14 full-H64 old-policy counterfactual credit, common scenario budget, λ=.5 assignment contrast and KL update. No imitation, pretraining or planner output enters training labels or inference.

## 3. Why this matches the simulator

The physical transition raises age by one on each unserviced tick, then draws an independent Bernoulli failure event whenever age reaches `failure_age`; service occupies a technician for the exact configured duration. The formula above computes an action-dependent chance of entering PM-to-CM failure **before any compatible worker could be released**, including specialists and long reciprocal assignments. V14's count penalty sees demand and occupation duration but does not explicitly couple each unserved machine's age threshold to the earliest compatible release. The V13 one-edge oracle did not maintain a hold, so it did not test a policy that repeatedly reprices exposure at each decision.

## 4. Nearest prior work and defensible boundary

[Learning to Dispatch](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/11958dfee29b6709f48a9ba0387a2431-Paper.pdf) already establishes RL dispatch with structured representations. [HetGPO/AirME](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/19839) already reasons over stochastic maintenance failures and resource allocation. [Dynamic traveling multi-maintainer policies with alerts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221724004284) already combine deep RL, engineer-centric actions and future asset condition in multi-maintainer dispatch. Earlier DRAS work already learns reservation/backfilling on reusable compute resources. V15 would claim, at most, that **this specific earliest-compatible-release PM-failure exposure proxy inside a scratch-RL joint-matching policy** helps under the fixed shared-technician simulator. It cannot claim invention of hazard-aware maintenance, resource reservation, RL dispatch or action-level lookahead. The limited primary-source check does not establish uniqueness of the exact feature; expand targeted prior-art review before any publication claim.

## 5. Falsifiable hypotheses and pre-implementation gate

First test on immutable V14 **training** Q states whether the exact `Z_n(a)-Z_n(WAIT)` feature is nondegenerate and differs materially from an age-agnostic release proxy in legal matching decisions, including target cases and both one/two-technician structures. Also test whether its ranking among same-cardinality action pairs better aligns with old-policy common-bank full-H64 Q than the control proxy, with all cases and seeds reported. This is exploratory because the Q bank uses old actor continuation; it cannot establish the new policy's closed-loop value. If feature differences are rare or misaligned, reject V15 before a full scratch-RL campaign.

If implemented, the primary fresh-seed test is lower equal-case H64 cost than an active-capacity-matched ablation in at least four of five training seeds, with a hierarchical CI upper <0 and improvement among strong control seeds; require a material effect (provisionally ≥10 cost units) and headroom against bounded rollout, flat PPO, prior structured RL, FCFS/risk, and a no-learned-feature diagnostic. A DEV result must be followed by independent scratch-RL replication and at most one prelocked fresh TEST under the unchanged A/B criteria. Failure to beat the matched control or planner headroom retires the method without opening TEST.

## 6. Ablation, information and compute controls

The principal control has the **same** base actor, trainable demand weights, positive scale, parameter count, current inputs, legal matchings and per-action release-time calculation, but replaces `Z_n(a)` by an **age-agnostic** bounded release proxy `B_n(a)=1-(1-p)^{D_n(a)}` for unserved machines, zero when started. Its score uses `B_n(a)-B_n(WAIT)`. This preserves hazard probability and release-time range but removes the coupling to each machine's remaining safe-age margin. Both arms are initialized identically and receive the same source-role seed schedule, four-scenario per-root Q budget, optimizer, updates, checkpoint rule and evaluation panel; realized on-policy states and root counts may diverge and must be reported. Both heads remain gradient-active at representative legal multi-action states. A separate existing V14 count control and strong baselines are evaluated on the same fresh physical panel. Report feature-opportunity frequency, decision classes, action cardinality/WAIT, per-case cost and failure/queue/availability, train/inference compute and numerical stability. The feature is an explicit inductive bias to test, not an imposed service rule.
