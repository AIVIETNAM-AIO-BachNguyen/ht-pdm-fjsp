# V10 proposal: within-machine-set counterfactual assignment credit

The audited V9 closed-loop intervention showed that changing technician assignment alone recovers ~86% of weak-replica reciprocal cost gaps. However that intervention is a shortest-duration heuristic overlay and still trails risk/rollout. V10 tests whether **learned** action credit, not a hard assignment restriction, can make the same legal-matching RL actor robust. This proposal is locked before implementation/outcomes; it may fail.

The controller remains centralized RL. At every tick it observes current machine condition/request/technician state (the V7 56-D current-information vector), samples or selects one full legal machine–technician matching including WAIT, and receives negative physical maintenance, unavailability and failure cost over H48 plus the fixed H16 tail. Its decision changes technician occupancy, other machines' service opportunities and later failure/queue costs. The threshold requester and feasibility mask are environment components; the actor chooses the dispatch. The four locked cases and objective stay unchanged.

**Existing V9 mechanism.** On-policy source states are sampled under the actor. A common-random full-H+tail forecast bank estimates Q_old(s,a) for every currently legal matching a, using a forced root then the same stochastic actor and fixed tail. V9 all-action expected policy gradient weights each action's advantage by its full matching probability. If a bad assignment sits inside a low-probability machine set, its within-set contrast receives little gradient. The V9 trace and intervention make this a plausible failure mechanism, not yet a proven training explanation.

**V10 method.** Keep the *same* V7 shared-edge actor, free legal mask and V9 all-action update. Partition feasible actions by the set S of machines served. Add an assignment-specific counterfactual improvement term on every S with at least two feasible technician assignments. For each S compute the conditional softmax of the actor's matching scores and paired-bank within-S return advantages `mean_{a in S} Q_old(s,a) - Q_old(s,a)` divided by 10. Maximize the expected within-S advantage under the new conditional policy, giving **equal weight to each eligible machine set** in a selected state, then average selected states. Weight this auxiliary term by fixed λ=0.5 relative to V9's all-action objective; retain the same old-to-new full-policy KL penalty and hard KL cap. Thus the extra term trains assignment preference even for sets the current actor rarely chooses. It uses Monte Carlo costs from the unchanged environment, not shortest-duration labels, planner actions or a feasibility mask. Nonshortest assignments remain selectable. Two common scenarios/action and the same complete Q bank feed both arms; no second simulator calls in the V10 arm. The ablation is exactly λ=0 under identical actor, data protocol and optimizer.

This is a structured use of known all-action/expected policy-gradient and conditional counterfactual credit ideas, **not** a general algorithm novelty claim. Prior expected policy-gradient work and graph-based shared-crew maintenance RL are acknowledged in V9. The research contribution being tested is whether partitioning joint matching returns by *served machine set* addresses a measured assignment failure in a shared-resource maintenance dispatcher while preserving learned subset/timing decisions and nonshortest actions.

**Risk and falsification.** Two-scenario Q estimates may be too noisy; uniform weighting can train on rare sets irrelevant to occupancy; the local edge scorer may lack needed global context; within-set preference can improve weak replicas yet leave strong replicas and machine subset choices unchanged. V10 is rejected as a competitive method if it only rescues weak replicas, fails the prelocked DEV gate, or a simple risk rule remains cheaper and faster. Test per-seed paired cost and assignment taxonomy, not only pooled mean. No TEST until a competitive DEV candidate and a separate confirmation protocol exist.
