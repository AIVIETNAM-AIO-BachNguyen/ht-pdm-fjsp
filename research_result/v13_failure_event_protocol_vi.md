# Prelocked V13 failure-event timing diagnosis on opened DEV

Locked after the independently audited V13 episode cost-component decomposition and while V14 DEV is RUNNING, before any event-level replay outputs are examined. This is a retrospective mechanism probe on the already opened V13 DEV panel, not a new controller, independent evaluation, replication, or TEST. It cannot promote V13 or V14.

## Question and hypothesis

In interchangeable scale1 and reciprocal scale2, V13 competition costs ~56 more than bounded rollout per episode almost entirely through extra failure cost, despite nearly equal H48 service starts. Does that excess arise mainly because a preventive request is already queued but upgrades to corrective before service, or because failure occurs before any preventive request exists? These are different timing/decision failure modes. A queued upgrade is observed in the simulator lifecycle as `event=upgraded`; a new corrective request after failure is `event=created, kind=corrective` at `tick+1`.

Use all four unchanged V13 cases as context. For every case, five selected competition checkpoints (training seeds 72500000–04) and the fixed 16 V13 DEV physical seeds 72510000–15, replay the deterministic H48 actor then unchanged H16 risk tail, and replay bounded rollout once per case/physical seed. Align rollout to each training seed only for paired differences. Validate every replay against the immutable V13 `episodes.csv` on objective, failures, service starts, queue/cost components and source hashes. Do not replay prior training, generate new physical seeds, inspect V14 partial outcomes or open TEST.

## Locked metrics and interpretation gate

Primary per-case quantities: mean H48 count per episode of (1) upgraded preventive requests and (2) newly created corrective requests, candidate minus rollout. Also report H48 total failures and the residual `H_failures - upgrades - new_corrective`, retaining any nonzero residual rather than forcing identity. For interchangeable and reciprocal2 separately, the *queued-upgrade explanation* is supported descriptively only if candidate-minus-rollout upgrades are positive in at least four of five training seeds and account for at least half the positive mean excess H48 failures. If either case fails, do not claim that queued PM lateness generally explains the planner gap. This is an event-class accounting gate, not causal evidence that a particular action would avert a failure.

Secondary fixed outputs by case/controller and paired seed: preventive and corrective service starts during H48; age and request wait at preventive start (mean over starts, with counts); age and wait of requests at upgrade; first failure tick; failure-event machine/tick overlap across paired trajectories; H48 service starts, availability, failures and H64 cost/failure cost. Event-level rows include lifecycle event, tick, case, physical seed, train seed/controller, machine, request kind and age/wait where defined. Do not use these to tune a method or select favorable states. Report the directional H48 metrics for all four cases, including reciprocal3 where V13 beats rollout, and seed variation for the two target cases. If event classes do not explain the gap, formulate another discriminating question rather than forcing a hazard architecture.

Paired hierarchical 20,000-draw 95% CI for each target case's upgrade difference: resample five training seeds with replacement, then the same 16 physical-seed indices within each sampled seed; bootstrap seed 72540001 (reserved only for this diagnostic, not a new physical trajectory). The primary published statistic remains the mean difference and five per-seed differences; CI is descriptive because the DEV episodes selected the actor checkpoints. No multiple fresh TEST attempts follow this probe.
