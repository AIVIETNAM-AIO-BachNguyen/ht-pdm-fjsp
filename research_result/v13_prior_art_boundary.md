# V13 prior-art boundary (primary-source check, before DEV verdict)

This note records a limited comparison, not a novelty claim or V13 outcome.

- Zhang et al., [*Learning to Dispatch for Job Shop Scheduling via Deep Reinforcement Learning*](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/11958dfee29b6709f48a9ba0387a2431-Paper.pdf), formulate a sequential eligible-operation decision in a disjunctive graph, use GIN policy embeddings, and optimize makespan through RL. They report size generalization. V13's stochastic maintenance requests, persistent technician occupation, and whole matching action are different modeling choices. Graph representation, RL dispatch and size-agnostic sharing are established ideas and are not V13 novelty.
- Wang and Gombolay, [*Stochastic Resource Optimization over Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks for Failure-Predictive Maintenance Scheduling*](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/19839/19839-40-23852-1-2-20220613.pdf), already study stochastic maintenance resource allocation in AirME. Their state summary and decision value nodes link scheduling choices to system state. The paper explicitly discusses balancing current resource use against future needs; its AirME implementation simplifies decision nodes because crews are homogeneous and leaves heterogeneous crews to later work. Thus V13 cannot claim to originate future-resource reservation or global maintenance context.
- The narrower V13 hypothesis is that an explicit occupation-duration × learned demand of *machines not served by each candidate matching* improves scratch RL dispatch with heterogeneous technician capabilities. This exact formula is not established by the two papers above, but this limited reading cannot establish uniqueness against broader shadow-price and scheduling literature. V13 requires its prelocked ablation, capacity-matched identity control if triggered, strong baselines, fresh confirmation and replication before a defensible method claim.

No generalization or scale claim follows from V13's shared encoders alone.

## Additional adjacent primary literature (2026-10-20 search)

- Ghosh et al., [*Neural-progressive hedging: Enforcing constraints in reinforcement learning with stochastic programming*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/ghosh22a.html), already combine an RL policy with stochastic programming at execution time for sequential resource allocation, including bike-sharing supply-demand matching. V13/V14 have no online planner or hedging correction at inference; their possible contribution cannot be the generic combination of RL and resource optimization.
- Jia, Shi and Shen, [*Online Learning and Pricing with Reusable Resources*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/jia22c.html), study queued demand and finite occupation of reusable resources using online pricing/bandits. This is a distinct model and not a direct maintenance-policy baseline, but it further rules out a broad claim that learning the future value of occupied reusable capacity originated here.
- Begnardi et al., [*Deep Reinforcement Learning for Two-sided Online Bipartite Matching in Collaborative Order Picking*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v222/begnardi24a.html), already apply end-to-end DRL and graph policies to uncertain worker-resource matching with strong heuristic comparisons. V13/V14 should not claim novelty in DRL-based bipartite matching or graph-based transfer.

These are adjacency checks from primary proceedings pages, not an exhaustive mechanism search. None of the abstracts above establishes or refutes prior use of the exact V14 occupation-duration × identity-of-unserved-machines term. The claim remains conditional on a fair identity ablation and an expanded targeted search before publication.

## Reservation boundary after audited V13 exposure diagnosis (2026-10-20)

The V13 opened-DEV exposure audit found more at-risk preventive machine-ticks while compatible technicians were already occupied in two planner-gap cases. This motivates a possible *earlier* commitment/reservation question, but it does not make resource reservation a new RL idea. Fan et al., [*Deep Reinforcement Agent for Scheduling in HPC*](https://cs.iit.edu/~lan/publications/DRAS-ipdps2021.pdf), explicitly train a hierarchical agent to choose immediate execution, reserved execution and backfilling of jobs on reusable compute nodes. AirME above already frames current maintenance allocations against future needs. Any future maintenance method must identify a narrower mechanism specific to stochastic PM-to-CM risk, heterogeneous technician compatibility and service-duration commitments, then show a fair scratch-RL ablation and strong-baseline advantage. The V13 exposure audit is observational and alone does not establish such a mechanism or novelty. This is a targeted adjacent primary-source check, not an exhaustive literature review.
