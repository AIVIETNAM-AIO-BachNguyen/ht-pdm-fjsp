# V13 prior-art boundary (primary-source check, before DEV verdict)

This note records a limited comparison, not a novelty claim or V13 outcome.

- Zhang et al., [*Learning to Dispatch for Job Shop Scheduling via Deep Reinforcement Learning*](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/11958dfee29b6709f48a9ba0387a2431-Paper.pdf), formulate a sequential eligible-operation decision in a disjunctive graph, use GIN policy embeddings, and optimize makespan through RL. They report size generalization. V13's stochastic maintenance requests, persistent technician occupation, and whole matching action are different modeling choices. Graph representation, RL dispatch and size-agnostic sharing are established ideas and are not V13 novelty.
- Wang and Gombolay, [*Stochastic Resource Optimization over Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks for Failure-Predictive Maintenance Scheduling*](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/19839/19839-40-23852-1-2-20220613.pdf), already study stochastic maintenance resource allocation in AirME. Their state summary and decision value nodes link scheduling choices to system state. The paper explicitly discusses balancing current resource use against future needs; its AirME implementation simplifies decision nodes because crews are homogeneous and leaves heterogeneous crews to later work. Thus V13 cannot claim to originate future-resource reservation or global maintenance context.
- The narrower V13 hypothesis is that an explicit occupation-duration × learned demand of *machines not served by each candidate matching* improves scratch RL dispatch with heterogeneous technician capabilities. This exact formula is not established by the two papers above, but this limited reading cannot establish uniqueness against broader shadow-price and scheduling literature. V13 requires its prelocked ablation, capacity-matched identity control if triggered, strong baselines, fresh confirmation and replication before a defensible method claim.

No generalization or scale claim follows from V13's shared encoders alone.
